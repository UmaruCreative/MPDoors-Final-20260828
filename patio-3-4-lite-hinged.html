Where does a hinged patio door take its wear?

Not at eye level. In the lower third.

The kicked ball, the dragged chair, the dog leaning its weight to get out first, and the panel carrying itself through the full arc of the swing.

The 3/4 Lite Hinged door glazes the upper three-quarters of each panel and backs the lower quarter with solid fiberglass, so the part built to take contact is the part doing the work. Ball-bearing hinges carry the swing, and a compression seal closes the same way at the thousandth use as at the first.

01 · The hinges

Ball-bearing hinges carrying the full swing

Two hundred fifty pounds a panel, on hinges built for the arc, not just the latch.

A standard hinge wears at the pin under repeated load. A ball-bearing hinge carries that load on bearings rather than bare metal, so the panel keeps closing flush to the frame instead of sagging at the latch side as the years pass.

Both panels use them, on every configuration in this line.

Hinges Ball-bearing, both panelsGlazing 3/4 lite · solid rail

02 · The seal & the hardware

A seal that closes the same way every time

Weatherstripping that brushes past a frame wears thin. This one is squeezed.

Compression weatherstripping closes against the frame under pressure rather than sliding past it, pressing tighter with use. Behind it, a polyurethane foam core; around it, a full composite frame that holds its shape through a humid summer and a hard freeze.

No handle arrives fitted, each panel is double bored at the factory, so the hardware you choose fits without further drilling.
