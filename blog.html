Measuring & installation

How to measure a rough opening for a replacement door

The door is ordered to the opening in the wall, not to the slab that is currently hanging in it.

A replacement door that will not close, or that closes but will not seal, is almost always an ordering problem rather than an installation problem. These are the measurements that decide it.

Take three widths

Measure the rough opening at the top, the middle and the bottom, from framing member to framing member, not from trim to trim. Record all three. The smallest of the three is the width you order to.

Take three heights

Measure from the sub-floor to the header on the left, in the centre and on the right. Again the smallest number governs. Measure to the sub-floor, not to the finished floor, unless the finished floor already runs through the opening.

Take both diagonals

Corner to corner, twice. If the two diagonals differ by more than about a quarter of an inch, the opening is out of square and wants correcting before the new unit goes in. A composite frame holds whatever geometry it is set in, which is an advantage when the opening is true and a permanent problem when it is not.

Note the wall thickness

Jamb depth has to match the wall. It is measured through the finished wall, from interior surface to exterior sheathing, and it is the measurement most often skipped.

Then check the swing

Standing outside, looking in: if the hinges are on your right and the door opens away from you, that is a right-hand inswing. Handing is not a preference, it is a dimension, and it cannot be changed after the unit is built.

Performance

U-Factor and SHGC, in plain terms

Two numbers, pulling in opposite directions, and the climate decides which one you optimise.

U-Factor

The rate at which heat escapes through the assembly. Lower is better, everywhere, in every climate. It is the number that matters most where winters are long.

SHGC

Solar Heat Gain Coefficient: the fraction of the sun's heat that gets through the glass. Lower means a cooler room in a Southern summer. Higher means free winter heat in a Northern one. There is no universally correct value, which is exactly why ENERGY STAR sets four different zones.

Why the zone matters

ENERGY STAR certifies windows and doors across four United States climate zones, and most manufacturers meet them with regional variants: different glass, different seals, different assumptions. MP Doors is certified across all four with one composite door.

Where to find the real numbers

Every configuration carries its own NFRC-certified figures, and they are printed on the specification table of each product page along with the label identifier. Where a figure is still pending confirmation, it says so.

Materials

Why freeze–thaw is a water problem, not a cold problem

Cold on its own does very little to a door. Cold plus water does all of it.

Moisture enters a joint as a liquid and leaves it as a solid nine percent larger. In a Northern winter that happens several hundred times a season, and each cycle is a small wedge driven into the same seam.

What it does to wood

Wood takes the water in through end grain, principally at the bottom rail where the cut is hidden and the coating was never asked to reach. It swells, then splits along the grain, then holds more water than it did before.

What it does to vinyl

Vinyl does not absorb, but it becomes brittle in sustained cold and loses its seal at the corner weld, which is the point under the most stress.

What a composite frame does instead

It cannot take the water in, so there is nothing to expand. The geometry set on installation day is the geometry that survives the winter, and the one after it. That is the whole argument, and it is a material argument rather than a coating argument.

Coastal

What HVHZ approval actually certifies

Three separate things get called "hurricane rated", and they are not interchangeable.

Design Pressure

A structural rating: how much positive and negative wind pressure the assembly withstands without failing. It says nothing about debris.

Impact and missile rating

A debris rating: whether the glazing survives a specified projectile at a specified speed, and then a cycling load afterwards. This is the one that matters when a hurricane turns loose material into a missile aimed at the opening.

State approval versus local code

A Florida product approval number, such as FL29714-R15, records that a product has been evaluated against the Florida Building Code. High-Velocity Hurricane Zone acceptance is a further, jurisdiction-specific matter, and it should always be confirmed against local code before ordering.

What MP Doors publishes

The impact series carries its Florida approval, its structural rating and its label identifiers on the product specification table, together with a note wherever a figure is pending confirmation rather than final.

Care

Caring for a factory finish that never needs refinishing

There is no maintenance cycle here, because there is no substrate underneath waiting to be exposed.

Three habits

  • Wash the faces with mild soap and water, and rinse. Nothing abrasive, no solvent.
  • Keep the sill and, on gliding doors, the track clear of grit. Grit is what wears a roller and a weatherstrip.
  • Check the weatherstrip once a season by closing the door on a sheet of paper: if it pulls out without resistance at any point along the seal, the unit wants adjusting.

One decision to take carefully

Repainting a factory finish is possible, but a dark colour absorbs considerably more heat than a light one. The warranty may be void if the factory finish is repainted below LRV 57, and it is void above 4,000 feet elevation. Both conditions are set out on the warranty page.

Design

Choosing decorative glass: light in, privacy kept

A decorative lite is a privacy decision that happens to be a design decision.

The question to ask of any pattern is not whether it is attractive, but how much of the hallway a visitor standing at the door can resolve.

The clear end

Low-E clear glazing maximises light and offers no obscuration. It suits a full-lite door onto a private garden, and a front entry with a solid porch screen in front of it.

Textured and patterned

Water Wave, Vintage and Transclear break up the image without cutting the light much. Shapes and movement remain readable; detail does not.

Decorative caming

Savana, Scotia, Andaman, Flores and Zen combine textured fields with a leaded pattern. Obscuration is highest where the pattern is densest, which is generally the centre, exactly where a sightline into a hallway would otherwise run.

Integral blinds

Mini blinds sealed inside the glazing give full-view glass and full privacy on demand, with no dusting. Coverage for glass with mini blinds is a shorter, prorated tier than for standard glass, which is worth knowing before choosing.
